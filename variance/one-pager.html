The problem

Part of my job in a distribution center is researching inventory discrepancies. Most of that time is recognizing patterns I have seen hundreds of times: the same part short in one slot and over in the next, a box of 12 counted as one, 64 keyed as 46. A tool should catch those first, so a person's time goes to the variances that do not fit a pattern.

What I built

  1. Measure both ways. Gross accuracy counts every dollar; net lets overs and shorts cancel.
  2. Prioritize by dollars. A Pareto ranking shows which few lines carry most of the gross.
  3. Classify the likely cause. Eight causes in a deliberate order, mislocated pairs first, so one move is not written up as two problems.
  4. Draft the write-up: observation, mechanism, implication, ready to verify.

The result

  • On a synthetic count of 164 lines with 14 problems planted in it, all 14 were classified correctly. A test checks this on every change.
  • It also found a pair I did not plant: a serpentine belt over 3 in one aisle and short 3 in another.
  • The same figures come from JavaScript in the browser, a pandas port and SQL queries, and tests check that they agree.

A drafted write-up, as Variance produces it

Observation. BAT-5100 (Battery, group 24F) at C-14-02: system 18, counted 6, a variance of −12 units (−$1,704.00). Mechanism. The same item is short in one location and over by about the same amount in another, which points to a put-away or replenishment move that went to the wrong slot. The offsetting variance is at C-15-04. Implication. Recount both locations and review the movement history. If the mislocation is confirmed, correct the locations through the appropriate transfer process and review the put-away that touched both slots.

The decision

Investigate the largest dollar differences first. Recount the affected locations and review movement records before accepting a suggested cause or posting an adjustment.

The limits

These are rules, not a model, and the tolerances are judgment calls. Evidence depends on what the export includes. It has only seen synthetic data; no employer data was used to build or test it.
