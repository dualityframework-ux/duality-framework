What would change the recommendation?

A matching overflow count removes a quantity loss only after item identity, ownership and location records agree. A dispatch record explains a shortage only after the period, revenue and cost postings are checked. A count difference alone establishes neither cause nor the final expense.

Before close, retain the signed recount, location search, movement history and posting review. The controller resolves remaining uncertainty and approves the period and accounts. A prior-period error or uncertain ownership would require a different analysis.

Accounting assumptions

This model uses a constant carrying cost and current-period events. It assumes no tax, freight, returns, valuation write-downs or cost-layer differences. Revenue stays fixed. For the shipment scenario, the sale’s revenue is already included in the starting balance.

For this illustration, confirmed inventory losses are included in COGS. If the reporting policy puts them below gross profit, the loss still reduces inventory and pre-tax profit, but would not reduce the gross margin shown here. Account classification requires review.

IAS 2’s overview explains expense recognition for sold inventory and inventory losses. That principle informs this example; it does not establish a company’s account mapping or jurisdiction-specific treatment.
