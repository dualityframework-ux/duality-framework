Why a 99% count can hide $11,000

Net inventory accuracy lets overages cancel shortages. Gross accuracy doesn't. The gap between them is where the real work is.

Ian Kachadorian · October 2026 · 4 min read

Two numbers from the same count

Take one cycle count: 164 locations, about $204,000 of stock on the books. Run it through Variance and you get two accuracy figures.

99.01%
Net accuracy
94.32%
Gross accuracy

Same count, same items, same day. One number says the building is nearly perfect. The other says about one dollar in eighteen is wrong.

What each one measures

Net accuracy adds up every variance with its sign. Overages are positive, shortages are negative, and they cancel.

net accuracy = 1 − | sum of variance dollars | ÷ book value = 1 − | +$4,777.90 − $6,784.35 | ÷ $203,651.85 = 1 − $2,006.45 ÷ $203,651.85 = 99.01%

Gross accuracy adds up every variance as a positive number. Nothing cancels.

gross accuracy = 1 − sum of | variance dollars | ÷ book value = 1 − ($4,777.90 + $6,784.35) ÷ $203,651.85 = 1 − $11,562.25 ÷ $203,651.85 = 94.32%

Net answers a finance question: how far off is the inventory value on the balance sheet? Gross answers an operations question: how much stock is not where the system says it is?

Where the cancelling happens

Two of the largest lines in this count show why the two diverge.

LocationItemUnitsDollars
C-15-04Battery, group 24F+12+$1,704.00
C-14-02Battery, group 24F−12−$1,704.00

Twelve batteries are in the slot next door. To net accuracy, that is zero: the plus and the minus cancel, and the books are right. To gross accuracy, it is $3,408 of variance, and to a picker it is a real problem. The next order for that battery goes to C-14-02, finds six instead of eighteen, and either short-ships or triggers a hunt.

Every mislocation works this way. So do look-alike swaps, where two similar items are each off by the same amount in opposite directions. They are invisible to net accuracy and fully visible to gross.

The rule of thumb: a big gap between net and gross accuracy usually means stock in the wrong place, not stock that is gone. Shrink pulls both numbers down together. Mislocation pulls down gross alone.

Which number to watch

Both. Report net to finance, because it is what the books care about. Run the floor on gross, because it is what pickers, replenishment and customers feel. And watch the gap: in this count it is nearly five points, and more than half of the gross dollars come from mislocated stock.

The cycle count program I ran covered 250,000+ locations a trimester, and we reported both: 97.5% gross and 99% net. The second number alone would have hidden most of the work. A team can hit 99% net while the floor is quietly getting harder to pick from.
